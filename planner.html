Activity: Building a Fire Station

Duration: 45 minutes Learning Area: Discovery of the World + Aesthetics & Creative Expression
Key Concepts: Community roles, cooperation, spatial awareness

Scaffolding Strategies

Main Activity

Kinesthetic Learners

Allow children to physically build the fire station using large hollow blocks. Incorporate movement: “run like firefighters” to count blocks needed.

Provocation

Visual-Spatial Learners

Provide blueprint templates and reference photos of fire stations. Use grid paper for children to design their station before building.

Main Activity

Advanced Verbal

Assign the “Dispatcher” role: they narrate what’s happening, call out instructions, and describe the building process to classmates.

Tuning In

Fine-Motor Needs

Provide larger, ergonomic building pieces. Use Velcro-attached components that require less precision but still allow participation.

Closure

Sensory-Avoidant

Offer noise-dampening foam blocks. Designate a quieter building zone away from the main activity hub.

Rehearse & Retrieve Prompt

“Recall last week’s transportation activity. Ask: ‘Remember when we learned about different vehicles? What vehicle do firefighters use? How is a fire engine different from a bus?’”

Assessment Indicators

  • ✓ Can the child identify the role of firefighters in the community?
  • ✓ Does the child collaborate effectively during group building?
  • ✓ Can the child follow a simple blueprint or plan?

Post-Lesson Evaluation

AI Suggested Actions Based on your evaluation
▶ Improve teaching: During the building phase, try using more open-ended questions like “What would happen if…?” to extend sustained shared thinking. Avoid directing the outcome; instead, scaffold the process.
▶ Follow up with children: Leo showed emerging leadership during cooperative building. In the next session, pair him with a child who needs peer support in turn-taking to reinforce this strength.
▶ Environment: Set up a “Community Helpers” station in the dramatic play corner with firefighter props, hats, and a phone for “emergency calls” to rehearse and retrieve vocabulary from today’s lesson.
